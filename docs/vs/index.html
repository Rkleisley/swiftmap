Comparison

swiftmap vs folium, deck.gl, kepler.gl and datashader

Five libraries that all put data on a map, separated by what they optimise for. folium and ipyleaflet have Leaflet's ergonomics but hit a ceiling in the tens of thousands of features. deck.gl scales, with its own layer and view-state model and a thin Python layer over it. kepler.gl is an application you configure rather than a library you call. datashader goes further than any of them on raw volume by rasterising server-side — which is also why what arrives in the browser is a picture rather than features you can click.

swiftmap keeps the Leaflet model and puts WebGL pipelines under it, so the map you write in a few add_* calls stays interactive at a million features. The table below includes the row where that trade-off loses.

Where it misses, honestly

A comparison table that wins every row is a pitch, not an analysis. These are the places another library is the better answer today. They are known, they are on the list, and this section shrinks as they land — it has already lost density, raster imagery, WMS, offline, marker clustering and line direction since it was first written.

3D

No extrusions, no terrain, no camera pitch. deck.gl owns this, and a Leaflet-shaped library will always be a step behind it here.

Vector tiles

read_mvt brings a vector-tile dataset in as an ordinary table — the bridge, not a renderer. There is still no vector-tile basemap, so a style-driven basemap has to come in another way.

Earth-observation catalogues

add_imagery warps a raster you already have. STAC, COG and Earth Engine integration is leafmap's territory, not this library's.

First paint at the top end

Millions of points drag smoothly once they are there. Getting them there still costs more than it should on the very largest maps.
